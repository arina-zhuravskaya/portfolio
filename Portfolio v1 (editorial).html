Greenpark · embedded at AstraZeneca / Feb 2026 — present / EMEA · Hybrid

Making medical data legible across seven markets.

As Graphic Designer & Production Associate, I translate complex clinical data into charts, decks, and HCP-facing surfaces — held to brand and regulatory compliance across seven EMEA markets.

RoleGraphic Designer & Production Associate
TeamGreenpark agency & AstraZeneca creative studio
SurfacesDecks · HCP portal · Knak email · print · social
ToolsFigma · PowerPoint · Adobe CC · Knak · Jira
Context

Pharma communication has a uniquely high cost of being misread. The job isn't to make charts pretty — it's to remove every ambiguity between a clinical insight and the person who has to act on it.

I joined Greenpark as the agency's embedded designer at AstraZeneca's Warsaw office. The brief: client-facing decks, educational and marketing presentations, and migrating website visuals into AZ Poland's HCP (Healthcare Professional) portal — all within the existing design system, all under brand and regulatory compliance.

Problem

Decks were arriving from analytical teams as dense, undifferentiated information. Charts didn't follow hierarchy principles; takeaways were buried two pages deep. Across seven markets — each with its own language, regulator, and brand nuance — small inconsistencies multiplied into trust issues.

Process

Cognitive-load & hierarchy first. Every chart was rebuilt around a single takeaway — what does this clinician need to act on? Secondary data lives at a lower visual weight. The eye finds the answer before it finds the noise.

HCP portal migration. Migrated and produced website visuals for AZ's Polish HCP portal within the established design system — colour, type, iconography, all held to a strict component library so no asset feels like an outlier.

Cross-channel campaigns. Designed print-ready materials and Knak email templates for brand initiatives across seven markets — including post and article designs for AZ Poland's Zwrotnik Raka (cancer awareness, 60K subscribers) and Nie Wyglądam (asthma awareness, 22K subscribers).

Concept work. Sketched a Microsoft Teams chatbot concept to streamline client → Creative Studio coordination — reducing the back-and-forth that ate into production time.

In one line
Charts aren't decorative — they're the shortest path between a number and a decision.
— Working note, sprint 03

60K reached Zwrotnik Raka cancer-awareness platform — post & article contributions

Angel Trade Co., Limited / Sep 2022 — Nov 2025 / Remote · Warsaw ⇄ Hong Kong

A three-year design partnership across a six-hour timezone.

From Graphic Designer to Junior Product Designer — I owned visual design end-to-end for an international trade & distribution company specialising in consumer electronics (notably Xiaomi partnerships), across four international trade shows and three continents.

RolesGraphic Designer → Junior Product Designer
Duration3 years, 3 months · Remote
ShowsITC Malta '25 · GITEX Dubai '24 · IFA Berlin '24 + '25
SurfacesTrade-show booth · merch · campaigns · e-commerce · print
Context

Trade shows are unforgiving. A 30-square-metre booth in a hall of 600 has to do three things at once: announce itself across an aisle, hold up to a five-second walk-past, and reward the visitor who stops to read.

I joined Angel Trade — a Hong Kong-based international trade and distribution company specialising in the global distribution of consumer electronics, known for partnerships with brands like Xiaomi — as a remote designer in Warsaw. Over three years I held the visual design across four flagship trade shows on three continents, while also delivering year-round campaign, packaging, and e-commerce work.

Part I — System

A modular trade-show design system that took booth design from one week to two days. A 70% time saving — same craft, less reinvention.

The brief kept repeating. Different show, different products, different KPIs — but the same components: a hero wall, a product table, a meeting nook, a screen-led demo zone. I built a modular system + brand guide that defined the parts and their reuse rules.

Booth design time dropped from a week to two days. The team could focus on what was actually new about each show — not on rebuilding the same wayfinding from scratch.

Part II — Trade shows

Four international shows. Same system, four expressions.

Owned visual design end-to-end — booth graphics, on-screen visuals shown to international attendees, presentation decks, motion graphics, signage, badges, and giveaways. Each show kept the system's spine but found a posture for the audience: Dubai aimed at trade buyers, Berlin at consumer-tech press, Malta at distributor relationships.

Part III — Merch & packaging

Branded notebooks, planners, packaging, gift sets — pieces a visitor takes home and keeps.

The best trade-show souvenir isn't loud — it's useful enough to live on a desk for a year. Designed merchandise that earned that shelf time.

Part IV — Campaigns

Cross-channel campaigns: banners, roll-ups, social posts and stories, email, magazine ads, paid social on Facebook, TikTok & Google.

Same system, eleven aspect ratios. Prepared e-commerce imagery and high-stakes print deliverables in parallel, holding visual consistency across every format the brand showed up in.

Reflection
The system isn't the work — the time it returns to the team is the work.
— After IFA Berlin 2024

§ 02

About.

A short, honest read

I'm Arina. I design at the seam where editorial discipline meets product thinking — and I want the work to be as clear to a clinician as it is to a kid listening to a podcast.

I grew up moving between languages — Belarusian, Russian, Polish, English, German, a little Japanese — and that's the lens I bring to design. Six different ways to say "hello" trains you to notice how something is said, not just what.

I've spent the last three years splitting time between commercial work for global brands (AstraZeneca, Xiaomi via Angel Trade) and independent cultural projects for the Belarusian diaspora. The two halves feed each other: trade-show systems taught me ruthless reuse; podcast identities taught me restraint.

I work best with people who care about craft and aren't precious about it. I read a lot. I take notes by hand.

№ 01 Behaviour over decoration. Every design decision earns its place by what it makes someone do or feel — not by how it looks in a portfolio shot.
№ 02 Build the system, then break it. Modular foundations cut a one-week booth design to two days at Angel Trade. The rules exist so you know what's worth breaking.
№ 03 Translate, don't simplify. Medical data, multi-language campaigns, children's fairy tales — different audiences, same job: keep the meaning, find the shape.
